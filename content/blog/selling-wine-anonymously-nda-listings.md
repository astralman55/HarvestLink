## Why would a winery sell wine without its name on it?

Most wineries protect their brand carefully. A winery known for a certain price and quality does not always want a surplus or off-blend lot showing up under its name at a lower price. So it sells that wine in bulk without attribution.

Common reasons include:

- **Brand and price positioning.** A discounted lot under the winery's name can confuse customers and distributors.
- **Contract or relationship reasons.** Some sellers prefer not to name the source of a lot.
- **Declassified wine.** A lot that did not go into a top blend or appellation label can still be excellent wine, and it is often sold anonymously.
- **Privacy.** Growers and small producers may not want their inventory to be public.

Buyers benefit too. Some of the most interesting lots, from established producers, are only available because the seller can stay anonymous.

## What does "selling under NDA" mean on BWG?

When a seller lists under NDA, BWG hides identifying details from buyers and from search engines.

| Hidden on an NDA listing | Still shown |
|---|---|
| Seller name, company, and username | Variety or varietal |
| Winery and vineyard name | Vintage or harvest year |
| Winemaker name | Quantity |
| Contact details | Price per ton or gallon |
| Exact location details | ABV and sulfites (bulk wine) |
| The listing title's sub-area detail | Farming practices |

Location is shown at the precision the seller chooses: either the county and region, or the state only. The listing page, the page title, the sitemap, and the alerts BWG sends all use the same redacted version.

## How does a buyer contact an anonymous seller?

The buyer sends an inquiry through the site. The seller gets it in their inbox and by email, and replies come back through BWG. The buyer sees a "Confidential Seller" label, not a name. The seller decides what to share and when.

## What does NDA not mean?

NDA on BWG is a **confidentiality setting on the listing**. It is not itself a legal agreement between two parties. If a seller wants a signed confidentiality agreement before revealing who they are, they can arrange one directly with the buyer, ideally with legal advice.

## Tips for sellers

- **Do not identify yourself in the description.** The listing form checks free text against your company name, username, and vineyard names, and blocks anything that would identify you.
- **Choose your location precision carefully.** In a small appellation, a county can point to a few wineries. Choosing "state only" gives the most privacy.
- **Use structured details.** ABV, sulfites, farming practice, and quantity build confidence without revealing you.
- **Preview the listing.** The form shows exactly what an anonymous buyer will see before you publish.
- **Have samples and lab analysis ready.** Buyers of anonymous lots rely on data.

## Tips for buyers

- **Ask for a sample from the actual lot** and independent or seller-provided lab analysis.
- **Get the details in writing.** Vintage, varietal, and quantity should match what the listing says.
- **Follow the steps in [how to buy bulk wine](/blog/how-to-buy-bulk-wine).** They apply equally to anonymous and public lots.
- **Ask what the seller will reveal and when.** Some sellers reveal their name once a serious buyer is ready to move.
