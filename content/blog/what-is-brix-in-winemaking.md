## What does brix mean?

Brix is a measure of how much sugar is dissolved in grape juice. One degree Brix corresponds to about one gram of sugar per 100 grams of juice. As grapes ripen on the vine, their sugar goes up and their acid goes down, so brix is the number that tells you how far along the fruit is.

## Why is brix such an important number?

Sugar is what yeast turns into alcohol during fermentation. That makes brix a direct predictor of how strong the finished wine could be, and a practical way to decide when to pick.

It is also the simplest number a grower and a buyer can agree on. A grape listing that states a brix target tells the buyer the grower's expectation for ripeness at harvest.

## How do you convert brix to alcohol?

A common rule of thumb is that potential alcohol by volume is brix multiplied by about 0.6. Sources give a range of roughly 0.55 to 0.65, because the yield depends on the yeast and the conditions.

| Brix at harvest | Approximate potential ABV (using 0.6) |
|---|---|
| 20 | about 12 percent |
| 22 | about 13 percent |
| 24 | about 14 percent |
| 26 | about 15.5 percent |

This is an estimate for a fully dry wine. If fermentation stops early or the winemaker adds or removes water or sugar, the result changes. For more on alcohol levels and labels, see [how much alcohol is in wine](/blog/how-much-alcohol-is-in-wine).

## How is brix measured?

- **Refractometer.** A few drops of juice on a prism give a reading in seconds, so it is the vineyard tool.
- **Hydrometer.** Floats in a cylinder of juice and reads density, commonly used in the cellar.

Sampling matters. Berries in the same block differ, so people sample many berries from many vines, not a single cluster.

## Is brix the only ripeness measure?

No. Sugar is one part of ripeness. Winemakers also look at:

- **Acidity and pH.** Acid drops as sugar rises, and the balance shapes freshness.
- **Flavor.** Tasting the berries is still standard practice.
- **Seed and skin maturity.** Especially for red wines, where tannin ripeness matters.
- **Berry condition and weather.** Rain or heat can force decisions.

A grower can chase a high brix number and still make unbalanced fruit, which is why targets are usually ranges, not a single point.

## What are typical brix targets?

Many table wines are picked in the low to mid 20s, though styles differ. Grapes for sparkling base wines are usually picked earlier and lower, and grapes for dessert wines can be much higher. Ask the grower what target they are working toward for the wine you want.

## Are there other ways to measure sugar?

Brix is the standard in the United States, but you will meet other scales:

| Scale | Where it is common | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Brix** | United States, Australia, much of the world | Percent sugar by weight |
| **Baumé** | France and parts of Europe | Potential alcohol, roughly, in a related unit |
| **Oechsle** | Germany and Austria | Density of the juice |

If you buy fruit or wine from a producer who uses another scale, ask them to convert it to Brix, and ask for the reading on the same kind of sample.

## Why does the brix number move between samples?

A vineyard is not uniform. Sun exposure, vine vigor, and berry size all vary. That is why a single reading can mislead. Good sampling practice includes:

- Sampling from many vines across the block, not one row
- Taking berries from the top, middle, and bottom of clusters and from both sides of the row
- Crushing enough berries to give a representative juice sample
- Sampling at the same time of day each time, because berries lose and gain water

For buyers, this is also why contracts describe how and where fruit is sampled at delivery. The reading at the weigh station is the one that counts.

## How does brix show up when you buy grapes?

- **Listing.** HarvestLink grape listings include a brix target so you can filter by it.
- **Contract.** Agreements often state a target range and how the price changes above or below it.
- **Delivery.** Fruit is sampled on arrival, and the reading becomes part of the record.
