## Step 1: What exactly do you need?

Write your spec before you talk to anyone. Sellers respond faster, and quotes are easier to compare, when you are specific.

- **Varietal or blend** (for example Cabernet Sauvignon, or a red blend)
- **Vintage**, or whether a multi-vintage blend is acceptable
- **Appellation or origin** you want on the label, if any
- **Style**: ABV range, oak, sweetness
- **Volume** in gallons, and whether you can take a partial lot
- **Budget** as a price per gallon, and a timeline

If you are new to the terms, read [what bulk wine is](/blog/what-is-bulk-wine) first.

## Step 2: Are you allowed to buy it?

Bulk wine is a trade product. In the United States, running a wine business generally means federal permission from TTB and licensing from your state. TTB states that anyone wishing to operate a wine premises must apply and receive permission before starting operations. What you need depends on whether you will store, blend, bottle, or resell the wine.

Confirm this before you agree to buy. It is general information, not legal advice, so talk to TTB, your state alcohol agency, or a qualified professional.

## Step 3: Where do you find sellers?

| Source | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace (like [BWG](/bulk-wine)) | Comparing many lots by spec and price per gallon | You arrange samples, payment, and delivery directly |
| Broker | Sourcing help and paperwork | Broker fees and less direct contact |
| Directly from a winery | Building a relationship | Fewer lots to compare |

Some of the strongest lots are listed confidentially. On BWG you can send an inquiry without ever learning the seller's name until they choose to share it. See [how NDA listings work](/blog/selling-wine-anonymously-nda-listings).

## Step 4: Get samples and lab analysis

Never buy on a description alone. Ask for a sample from the actual lot and lab analysis on the same lot. Useful numbers include:

- **ABV** (alcohol by volume)
- **Total SO₂** (see [sulfites explained](/blog/sulfites-in-wine))
- **Volatile acidity (VA)**
- **pH and titratable acidity (TA)**
- **Residual sugar**
- Whether **malolactic fermentation** is finished

Ask whether the sample and the tank are the same wine, and when the analysis was done.

## Step 5: Agree on terms in writing

Because BWG does not process payments or handle shipping, you and the seller arrange the deal directly. Put it in writing. A clear agreement covers:

1. Exact wine, vintage, and quantity in gallons
2. Price per gallon and total price
3. **FOB or delivered**, and who books and pays for transport
4. When and how payment is made
5. Pickup or delivery dates
6. Who pays for testing, and what happens if the wine does not match the sample
7. Any taxes or fees, and who is responsible

## Step 6: Plan the logistics

Bulk wine moves by tanker truck, tote, barrel, or flexitank. Clean, food-grade containers matter, and so does temperature. Confirm that your receiving facility can accept the load, and match the pickup schedule to the seller's cellar schedule.

## Step 7: Check the wine when it arrives

Sample the delivered wine before you accept it and compare it to the original sample and lab numbers. If something is off, tell the seller right away and follow the process you agreed on.

## 20 questions to ask a bulk wine seller

1. What is the exact varietal and vintage?
2. What percentage of each variety and vintage is in the blend?
3. What appellation can be claimed, and how do you know?
4. What is the ABV?
5. What is the total SO₂?
6. What is the VA, pH, and TA?
7. Is malolactic fermentation complete?
8. Is the wine stable, fined, and filtered?
9. Has it been in oak, and for how long?
10. Are the farming practices certified, and can you share documents?
11. How many gallons are available, and what is the minimum?
12. Where is the wine stored?
13. Is the price FOB or delivered?
14. Can I have a sample from the actual lot?
15. Can I see recent lab analysis?
16. Is the wine in bond or taxpaid?
17. How soon can it be picked up?
18. What are the payment terms?
19. What happens if the delivered wine does not match the sample?
20. Are there other lots available from the same source?
