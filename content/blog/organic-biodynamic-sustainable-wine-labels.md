## Why do these labels confuse people?

The words sound alike, but they mean different things. Some are backed by a certifier who audits the farm. Some are programs with their own rules. One, "natural," has no legal definition at all. If you buy grapes or bulk wine, the difference matters because you may want to make label claims, or your customers may expect them.

## Quick comparison

| Label | Certified? | What it covers | What to ask the seller |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Organic** | Yes (USDA National Organic Program) | Farming without most synthetic pesticides and fertilizers; wine has its own rules | Certificate, certifier, scope, expiry |
| **Made with organic grapes** | Yes | Organic grapes; winemaking allows some added sulfites | Whether the grapes and the wine are covered |
| **Biodynamic** | Yes (Demeter) | Organic practices plus biodynamic standards | Demeter certificate and the vineyard covered |
| **Regenerative organic** | Yes (Regenerative Organic Certified) | Builds on organic and adds soil health, animal welfare, and worker fairness | Certificate and which pillars were audited |
| **Sustainable** | Varies | Programs such as SIP Certified, Lodi Rules, and Napa Green, or self-declared | Which program, and is there a certificate |
| **Natural** | No | No legal definition | What exactly they mean |

## What does organic wine mean?

The USDA National Organic Program has two main categories for wine:

- **Organic wine** is made from organic grapes with no added sulfites. It can carry the USDA organic seal.
- **Made with organic grapes** is made from organic grapes and can have added sulfites, up to 100 parts per million. It cannot use the USDA organic seal.

Organic certification covers both the farming and the winemaking, so a wine is not organic just because the grapes were. For sulfite basics, read [sulfites in wine](/blog/sulfites-in-wine).

## What does biodynamic mean?

Biodynamic farming treats the farm as a whole system and uses specific preparations and practices. In the United States, the main certification is **Demeter**. Demeter certification builds on organic practices and adds requirements that are specific to biodynamic farming. Like organic, certification is verified by a third party. You may see "Demeter Certified Biodynamic" or "made with biodynamic grapes" depending on what is covered.

## What does regenerative organic mean?

**Regenerative Organic Certified** is a newer standard that requires organic certification as a base and adds requirements around soil health, animal welfare, and social fairness. It is audited by a third party, like the other certifications.

## What does sustainable mean?

It depends entirely on who says it. Some programs are audited and have detailed standards, such as **SIP Certified**, **Lodi Rules**, and **Napa Green** in California. Others are internal claims. If a seller says "sustainable," ask which program, and whether there is a certificate.

## What does natural wine mean?

There is no legal definition in the United States. People generally use it for wine made with minimal intervention, such as native yeast and low or no added sulfites, but there is no shared rule. Ask the seller to describe exactly what they do.

## Are these claims verified on BWG?

BWG bulk wine listings can select organic, biodynamic, natural, sustainable, regenerative organic, and Demeter certified biodynamic. Grape listings can select conventional, sustainable, organic, or biodynamic. These are **declared by the seller** and BWG does not verify them. Ask for documentation before you rely on a label.

This article describes farming and labeling, not health. Organic or biodynamic status does not make a wine better for you or worse for you.

## Buyer checklist

1. Which claim exactly, and is it certified?
2. Who is the certifying body?
3. What is the scope (vineyard, winery, both)?
4. What is the certificate's expiry date?
5. Does it cover the specific lot and vintage you are buying?
6. Can they provide traceability from vineyard to tank?
